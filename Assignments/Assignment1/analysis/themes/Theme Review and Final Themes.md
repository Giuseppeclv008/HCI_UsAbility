# Theme Review and Final Themes – Full Remote Work Under 35

## Introduction

To complete the thematic analysis of the needfinding data, we reviewed the six candidate themes previously identified from the interviews and examined them alongside the codes extracted from the questionnaire responses.

The interview analysis produced 66 descriptive codes (C01–C66), which were organized into six candidate themes. The questionnaire analysis produced 56 descriptive codes (S01–S56) from 158 excerpts collected from 15 respondents.

Rather than developing a separate set of candidate themes from the questionnaire, we used its codes to review and refine the thematic structure initially developed from the interviews.

The purpose of this review was to identify recurring patterns across the two sources, examine similarities and differences between participants’ experiences, and determine whether the initial themes adequately represented the collected data.

The review resulted in seven final themes. The following sections document the decisions made during this process, the resulting thematic structure, and the evidence supporting each theme.

## Reviewing the Interview Candidate Themes

The review began with the six candidate themes previously identified through the interview analysis.

We examined the codes and supporting excerpts associated with each theme to assess whether they represented a coherent pattern and whether the boundaries between themes were sufficiently clear.

We then considered the questionnaire codes to identify additional evidence, contrasting experiences, and aspects that were not adequately represented in the initial thematic structure. The questionnaire codes were used to refine the existing themes and identify patterns that had not emerged clearly from the interviews alone.

During this process, some themes were retained and refined, one was divided into two more specific themes, and another was dissolved. A new theme was also introduced to represent concerns about the stability and continuity of remote work.

## Main Changes to the Initial Structure

The most significant changes concerned the initial themes *Relationships That Require Intentional Effort* and *The Rhythm of the Day*.

The first was divided into two themes to distinguish relationships with remote colleagues from social life outside work. The second was dissolved because its codes were more meaningfully connected to other themes, as detailed in Table 2.1.

The review also led to the introduction of *Precarious Remote Work* (T7), which captures concerns about the stability of remote working arrangements and the possibility of being required to return to the office. This pattern emerged from the combined consideration of interview and questionnaire evidence.

## Integrating Questionnaire Codes

The questionnaire codes (S01–S56) were examined alongside the interview codes to assess whether the initial thematic structure adequately represented the experiences collected from both sources.

Rather than developing separate candidate themes from the questionnaire, we used its codes to identify similarities, additional perspectives, and contrasting experiences. This allowed us to strengthen existing themes, reconsider their boundaries, and identify aspects that were not sufficiently represented in the interview analysis.

The questionnaire codes contributed to the review in three main ways:

* **Supporting existing themes:** questionnaire responses provided additional evidence concerning work-life boundaries, communication with colleagues, social relationships, the home working environment, and the benefits of remote work.
* **Refining thematic distinctions:** the comparison helped distinguish experiences that were related but conceptually different, particularly professional relationships and social life outside work.
* **Identifying an additional pattern:** questionnaire responses concerning the possibility of losing remote work, mandatory office returns, and uncertainty about future working arrangements contributed to the development of *Precarious Remote Work* (T7).

The questionnaire codes were therefore integrated into the revised thematic structure rather than treated as a separate set of themes. The resulting seven themes are presented in the following section, together with their definitions and supporting evidence from both sources.

## Final Thematic Structure

The review of the interview candidate themes, supported by the questionnaire codes, resulted in seven final themes. Together, they represent the main patterns identified in the experiences of fully remote workers.

Each theme was organized into subthemes to distinguish specific aspects of the broader pattern.

## Defining and Naming the Final Themes

Following the review process, the seven final themes were defined and named to represent distinct aspects of fully remote working experiences. Each theme was organized into subthemes to clarify the different patterns identified in the data.

The interview codes (C01–C66) and questionnaire codes (S01–S56) were considered together to identify supporting evidence, additional perspectives, and contrasting experiences. Only questionnaire codes with a clear and direct connection to a subtheme were included.

### T1 – Flexible Boundaries Between Work and Life

This theme explores how remote workers manage the separation between professional responsibilities and personal life. While remote work offers greater flexibility, it can also make boundaries less clearly defined. Participants described different approaches, ranging from establishing specific routines and limits to deliberately allowing work and personal activities to overlap.

#### a. Strategies and Structures for Disconnecting

Workers use routines, dedicated spaces, digital tools, and personal or organizational rules to establish boundaries and disconnect from work. These strategies may develop over time as workers become more familiar with managing their working arrangements.

*Interview codes: C01, C07, C14, C16, C21.*

*Questionnaire codes: S01, S08, S09, S11, S12, S14, S47.*

#### b. When Work Spills Over

Work can extend into personal time because of deadlines, unexpected events, or communication across different time zones. These situations may generate stress, anxiety, or difficulties disconnecting from professional responsibilities.

*Interview codes: C02, C05, C10, C13, C15, C18, C19, C20.*

*Questionnaire codes: S15, S17, S18.*

#### c. Permeability as a Choice

Some workers intentionally maintain flexible boundaries, accepting occasional overlaps between professional and personal activities in exchange for greater autonomy. Such overlaps are not necessarily experienced negatively, particularly when workers retain control over their schedules and availability.

*Interview codes: C03, C04, C06, C17.*

*Questionnaire codes: S13, S16, S19, S20.*

### T2 – Asking for Help at a Distance

This theme focuses on the challenges of requesting assistance and accessing support when colleagues are not physically present. Remote communication can make it harder to identify the right person to contact, understand their availability, or ask questions without feeling intrusive. These difficulties can be particularly relevant during onboarding.

#### a. Reaching the Right People

Workers may need additional time and effort to identify and contact colleagues who can provide information or assistance. Without immediate face-to-face interaction, obtaining a response can require follow-up messages or scheduled calls.

*Interview codes: C31, C34, C37.*

#### b. The Fear of Disturbing Others

Some workers hesitate to ask questions because they are uncertain about colleagues’ availability or worry about interrupting their work. This hesitation may encourage them to solve problems independently, even when asking for help would be more effective.

*Interview codes: C32, C33, C40.*

This subtheme was retained despite being supported by only two interviews. It therefore represents a specific experience rather than a pattern that can be generalized to all participants.

#### c. Fragile Onboarding

Learning tools, understanding team practices, and becoming familiar with an organization can be more difficult without informal face-to-face interactions. Inadequate digital infrastructure and organizational practices may increase these difficulties, while remote mentoring can provide valuable support.

*Interview codes: C38, C39, C41.*

*Questionnaire codes: S49, S53.*

### T3 – Distant Colleagues, Slowly Built Bonds

This theme describes how professional relationships develop when colleagues interact primarily through digital channels. Although remote work can support effective collaboration, it may limit opportunities for informal conversations and personal familiarity. At the same time, some workers appreciate having fewer unnecessary or forced interactions.

#### a. Cordial but Distant Relationships

Interactions between colleagues may remain focused on work-related activities, making it harder to develop deeper familiarity and personal connections. However, reduced informal interaction can also be perceived positively when it limits distractions or unnecessary social obligations.

*Interview codes: C35, C36, C42, C44, C45, C47.*

*Questionnaire codes: S38, S40.*

#### b. Meeting in Person

Face-to-face meetings provide opportunities to strengthen professional relationships and establish connections that may be more difficult to develop through digital communication alone. Some workers therefore value occasional opportunities to meet colleagues in person.

*Interview codes: C43, C52, C53, C59.*

*Questionnaire code: S39.*

### T4 – Building a Social Life Intentionally

This theme concerns the effort required to maintain social relationships outside work. Without commuting and everyday office interactions, remote workers may have fewer spontaneous opportunities to meet other people. Maintaining an active social life can therefore require greater personal initiative, although the experience also depends on existing relationships, individual preferences, and the surrounding community.

#### a. Building a Social Network Outside Work

Workers maintain or develop social relationships through friendships, shared interests, local communities, and activities outside their professional environment. Existing social networks, coworking spaces, and other opportunities for interaction can help support social well-being, while geographical limitations may make building new connections more difficult.

*Interview codes: C46, C49, C51, C64.*

*Questionnaire codes: S27, S28, S29, S30, S31, S33, S56.*

#### b. The Risk of Isolation

Spending extended periods at home can reduce opportunities for social interaction and contribute to feelings of isolation. However, this experience is not universal: some workers are comfortable spending time alone, while others associate loneliness with relocation, environmental conditions, or changing personal responsibilities rather than remote work itself.

*Interview codes: C48, C50.*

*Questionnaire codes: S32, S34, S35, S36, S37.*

### T5 – When Home Becomes the Office

This theme examines how the domestic environment influences the experience of remote work. Working from home requires adapting available spaces to professional activities, while physical comfort, workspace organization, and the presence of other people or disturbances can affect concentration and well-being.

#### a. Space and Physical Well-Being

The availability and organization of a suitable workspace, together with ergonomic equipment, physical comfort, and opportunities for movement, influence the working experience. Prolonged sitting and reduced daily movement may also create physical discomfort.

*Interview codes: C09, C22, C23, C24, C25, C26.*

*Questionnaire codes: S50, S51.*

#### b. Other People at Home

Living with family members, partners, or other people can introduce interruptions and require negotiation over shared spaces and working time. External disturbances, such as noise or maintenance work, may also interfere with concentration and meetings.

*Interview codes: C27, C28, C29, C30, C62.*

*Questionnaire code: S54.*

### T6 – Freedom and Work–Life Balance

This theme describes the opportunities remote work provides for organizing everyday life around personal needs and priorities. The absence of commuting and the possibility of working from different locations can create additional time and flexibility, supporting personal activities, family responsibilities, and greater freedom in choosing where to live.

#### a. Time Regained

Remote work eliminates or reduces commuting time, allowing workers to dedicate more time and energy to personal activities, household responsibilities, and well-being. The possibility of organizing certain activities during the working day can also contribute to a more flexible daily routine.

*Interview codes: C08, C54.*

*Questionnaire codes: S06, S21, S22.*

#### b. Location and Family

The possibility of working remotely can support geographical flexibility and make it easier to coordinate professional responsibilities with family and personal life. Workers may choose where to live, remain close to loved ones, or spend more time with household members.

*Interview codes: C55, C56, C57.*

*Questionnaire codes: S07, S23, S24.*

### T7 – Precarious Remote Work

This theme captures uncertainty about the stability and continuity of remote working arrangements. Although remote work provides significant benefits, some workers express concerns about whether these arrangements will remain available in the future. The possibility of being required to return to the office can influence how confidently they organize their personal and professional lives.

#### a. Fear of Losing Remote Work

Workers express concerns about losing remote working arrangements or being required to adopt less flexible working models. The absence of contractual guarantees and the possibility of mandatory office attendance can create uncertainty about future living and working conditions.

*Interview code: C65.*

*Questionnaire codes: S42, S46.*

#### b. The Relationship with the Office

Workers express different preferences and expectations regarding office attendance, including the importance of geographical proximity and the desire to preserve remote work as a working condition. Some would consider hybrid arrangements under specific circumstances, while others strongly oppose returning to the office.

*Interview code: C60.*

*Questionnaire codes: S43, S44, S45.*
